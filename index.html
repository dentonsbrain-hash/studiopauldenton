About

Paul Denton is a British designer and artist creating collectible objects that explore the intersection of design, narrative, and cultural mythology.

He studied Product Design at the University of Northampton before completing a Master's degree in Vehicle Design at the Royal College of Art. Denton spent close to a decade working at Ron Arad's studio, where he developed a deep understanding of the relationship between design, art, and gallery culture. Working as both a designer and digital sculptor, he translated Arad's sketch-led approach into resolved three-dimensional forms, gaining first-hand experience of how experimental design operates within the art world — from studio practice through to gallery presentation and collectible editions.

Following this, Denton has spent over eleven years at GA Design Group, contributing to award-winning international hospitality projects. Working across design and visualisation, he led teams in the development of spatial concepts and high-end environments, refining a precision-led approach to form, narrative, and presentation and visual narratives for complex, large-scale environments.

His current practice explores objects as artefacts — positioned between functional design and speculative narrative, the space between the rational and the speculative. Influenced by thinkers such as Rupert Sheldrake, Jacques Vallee and Dianna Pasulka, as well as ideas surrounding consciousness, perception, and the origins of creativity, Denton's work draws from a wide field spanning science, philosophy, and belief systems. Recurring themes include unidentified aerial phenomena, technological mythology, and the parallels between contemporary culture and ancient systems of meaning.

Denton's independent work is driven by a long-standing interest in where ideas originate. Influenced by figures such as Rupert Sheldrake, alongside broader questions around consciousness, perception, and belief, his practice explores the space between the rational and the speculative. Themes of UFO phenomena, scientific theory, and religious symbolism recur throughout his work, forming a framework through which objects become carriers of narrative and meaning.

Through limited editions and material-driven processes, his work investigates how objects can operate simultaneously as design, evidence, and cultural signal.
